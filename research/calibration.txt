The morning market opens at seven. Vendors set out apples, bread, and folded cloth along the north wall, and the street fills with ordinary errands. A short article in the local paper describes how a river cuts a valley, how sediment settles downstream, and how a town measures rainfall across a season. Later, a teacher copies a paragraph about library catalogs onto the board and asks the class to label each line: author, title, shelf mark, date.

People compare prices, check a bus timetable, and walk home under a grey sky. In the evening a neighbor repairs a bicycle chain, boils water for tea, and reads the weather note on the refrigerator. Nothing in the note is unusual: clouds, a light wind, and a chance of rain after midnight. The streetlights come on one by one, and the shops close in the order they always have.

The next day is warmer. A crew repairs the curb outside the bakery, and the noise carries two streets away. Someone waters the planters by the crossing, and a dog waits patiently while its owner talks to the baker. The library receives three boxes of returned books, and the catalog clerk checks each spine against the list before shelving them. A student asks for a map of the coast and is shown a drawer of folded charts, some printed forty years ago.

In the afternoon a meeting is held about the bus route. The discussion is calm and detailed: how many people use the stop near the school, how long the detour takes, and whether the schedule can change without affecting the first service of the day. Nobody agrees on everything, and the meeting ends with a list of questions for the next week.

By evening the wind has turned. The market stalls are folded and stacked, the street is swept, and the square is quiet except for the sound of a door being locked. A bus passes with its interior lights on, carrying four passengers and a driver who has worked this route for eleven years. The weather note is correct: the rain begins after midnight, lightly at first, then steadily, and by morning the gutters are full and the air smells of wet stone.
